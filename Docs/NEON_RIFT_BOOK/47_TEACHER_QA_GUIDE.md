# 47 · Teacher Q&A Guide (viva cheat sheet)

### 1. "What is Neon Rift?"
**SHORT:** A Unity 6 URP cyberpunk racing-heist game: drive into a generated night city, steal a data core in multi-stage hacks, and escape a city-wide lockdown before two AI rival crews.
**DETAILED:** One mission, Night Run, with 4 data-driven objectives, 3 player cars, 2 rival crews running their own heists, heat and lockdown consequences, an 84 s real-time cinematic intro and a graded debrief.
**FILES:** `D/Missions/Mission_NightRun.asset`, `S/Gameplay/Mission/MissionDirector.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [01](01_PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md)

### 2. "How is the project architected?"
**SHORT:** A persistent Bootstrap scene with one composition root, additive content scenes that receive a `GameContext`, ScriptableObject data, and missions that communicate by string event ids.
**DETAILED:** `GameRoot` creates the services; `GameFlow` loads one content scene at a time and calls `ISceneEntryPoint.Enter(context)`. There are no singletons (domain reload is disabled). 13 assemblies with one-way dependencies.
**FILES:** `S/Game/GameRoot.cs`, `S/Game/GameFlow.cs`, `S/Game/GameContext.cs` · **CLASS/METHOD:** `GameFlow.TransitionAsync` · **CHAPTER:** [02](02_ARCHITECTURE.md), [06](06_BOOTSTRAP_AND_SCENES.md)

### 3. "How did you make the city?"
**SHORT:** It is generated by an editor tool from layout data; nothing is hand-placed in the scene.
**DETAILED:** `CityLayout` lists roads, lots, reserved areas, heroes and districts. `Decompose` turns the road lines into a grid, and union-find merges cells into blocks. `NightRunDistrict` then builds the ground, markings, podium street walls, towers, props, landmarks, skyline and reflection probes; `CityInfill` fills block interiors. Meshes are combined per material per block. One menu rebuilds everything in about 40 s.
**FILES:** `S/Editor/District/CityLayout.cs`, `NightRunDistrict.cs`, `NightRunBuilder.cs` · **METHOD:** `NightRunBuilder.Build`, `CityLayout.Decompose` · **CHAPTER:** [07](07_CITY_AND_WORLD_GENERATION.md)

### 4. "How did you generate the buildings?"
**SHORT:** Three layers: procedural podium street walls, Sketchfab towers placed from a catalog, and procedural block infill.
**DETAILED:** `NightRunDistrict.BuildStreetWall` emits shopfront podiums with generated textures and a pixel font for signs. `BuildingPrefabBuilder` wraps the Sketchfab models into `PF_Bld_*` prefabs with `BuildingDefinition` data, and `PlaceHeroes` / `FillTowers` place them. `TowerDressing` + the `NeonRift/TowerFacade` shader add lit windows, crowns and aviation lights. `CityInfill` partitions free space with a largest-rectangle algorithm into yards, car parks, parks and back buildings.
**FILES:** `S/Editor/District/NightRunDistrict.cs`, `CityInfill.cs`, `TowerDressing.cs`, `S/Editor/World/BuildingPrefabBuilder.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [08](08_BUILDINGS_AND_ENVIRONMENT.md)

### 5. "How do the roads and routing work?"
**SHORT:** The same road list produces the visible roads and a runtime graph; Dijkstra finds routes, and closed gates make edges impassable.
**FILES:** `S/World/RoadGraph.cs`, `S/World/RoadNetwork.cs`, `S/Gameplay/Navigation/CityNavigation.cs` · **METHOD:** `RoadGraph.FindPath`, `CityNavigation.EdgeCost` · **CHAPTER:** [09](09_ROADS_AND_ROUTE_NETWORK.md)

### 6. "How does the vehicle physics work?"
**SHORT:** A custom Rigidbody car at 100 Hz with raycast suspension, a combined-slip tyre model, an engine/clutch/gearbox drivetrain, anti-roll bars, aero, ABS and traction control. No `WheelCollider`.
**DETAILED:** `VehicleController.Step`: suspension contact (5 rays per wheel) → springs, dampers, bump stops and anti-roll → steering → drivetrain → tyre forces per wheel (implicit wheel-spin integration) → differentials → aero → traction control → telemetry. All tuning comes from a `VehiclePhysicsProfile` in physical units.
**FILES:** `S/Vehicles/Physics/VehicleController.cs`, `VehicleWheel.cs`, `TyreModel.cs`, `Drivetrain.cs` · **METHOD:** `VehicleController.Step` (line 163) · **CHAPTER:** [11](11_VEHICLE_PHYSICS.md)

### 7. "How does the suspension work?"
**SHORT:** Per-wheel raycast springs whose rate comes from a natural frequency, with digressive dampers, bump stops and anti-roll bars.
**DETAILED:** `VehicleWheel.UpdateContact` casts 5 rays across the tyre profile; `SuspensionForce` = spring (preloaded to the model's ride height) + `DamperForce` (separate bump and rebound, linear to a knee then softer) + a progressive bump stop; `ApplySuspension` adds anti-roll = stiffness × compression difference per axle.
**FILES:** `S/Vehicles/Physics/VehicleWheel.cs`, `AxleSettings.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [11](11_VEHICLE_PHYSICS.md)

### 8. "How do the tyres work?"
**SHORT:** Slip ratio and slip angle are normalised by their peaks and combined; the force follows a sine rise and Gaussian fall, limited to a friction ellipse, with load sensitivity.
**FILES:** `S/Vehicles/Physics/TyreModel.cs` · **METHOD:** `TyreModel.Force`, `ClampToEllipse` · **CHAPTER:** [11](11_VEHICLE_PHYSICS.md)

### 9. "How do cars get their sound?"
**SHORT:** Telemetry-driven layered loops: 5 rpm points × on/off load, crossfaded equal-power in log-rpm and pitched by rpm. All synthesised in-house from a physical exhaust model.
**FILES:** `S/Audio/VehicleAudio.cs`, `EngineSoundModel.cs`, `S/Editor/Audio/EngineSynth.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [12](12_VEHICLE_AUDIO.md)

### 10. "How does car selection work?" / "How do you switch between cars?"
**SHORT:** `CarSelectScreen` browses the `VehicleCatalog`; `VehicleShowroom` swaps the car on a turntable with a light, camera and engine-blip transition; Confirm stores the choice in `RunSession` and plays the garage departure.
**DETAILED:** Previous/Next → `Select` → `VehicleShowroom.Show` (whip-out and spin-in) and `SetStats` (counting bars). Confirm → `RunSession.SelectVehicle(car, catalog)` (the other cars become rivals) → `VehicleShowroom.Depart` → `Flow.StartMission()`.
**FILES:** `S/Frontend/CarSelectScreen.cs`, `VehicleShowroom.cs`, `S/Game/RunSession.cs` · **METHOD:** `CarSelectScreen.Confirm`, `VehicleShowroom.Swap`, `Depart` · **CHAPTER:** [15](15_CAR_SELECTION.md)

### 11. "Where does the chosen car come from in the mission?"
**SHORT:** `MissionSceneEntry.Enter` spawns `Session.SelectedVehicle` through `VehicleSpawnPoint.Spawn`, which instantiates the prefab and applies the physics and audio profiles.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/MissionSceneEntry.cs`, `VehicleSpawnPoint.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [10](10_VEHICLE_SYSTEM.md)

### 12. "How does the rival AI work?"
**SHORT:** Each rival is a `RacerDriver` that feeds the same input interface as the player: it plans over the road graph, follows a racing line with pure pursuit and a braking-distance speed planner, sweeps ahead for traffic, yields, follows and recovers when stuck. `RivalDirector` gives each crew its own heist tasks and computes standings and qualifying.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Racing/RacerDriver.cs`, `RivalDirector.cs`, `RacingLine.cs`, `RacerProfile.cs`, `S/Missions/RivalTask.cs` · **METHOD:** `RacerDriver.ReadInput`, `RivalDirector.BeginTask`, `UpdateStandings` · **CHAPTER:** [17](17_RIVAL_AI.md)

### 13. "Do the rivals cheat?"
**SHORT:** No teleporting or fake forces while driving. The only non-physical action is the same Recover the player has, used out of view after repeated failed reversals. A bounded rubber band changes their target speed by at most about ±5–6 %.
**METHOD:** `RacerProfile.RubberBand`, `RacerDriver.Reset`, `InPlayerView` · **CHAPTER:** [17](17_RIVAL_AI.md)

### 14. "How does traffic work?"
**SHORT:** 14 roaming and 26 parked physical pack cars with spring suspension and lateral grip; they keep their distance, give way at junctions to the player and rivals, pull over in a lockdown, and are recycled out of view.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/City/CityTraffic.cs`, `TrafficCar.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [18](18_TRAFFIC_SYSTEM.md)

### 15. "How does the mission system know which objective is active?"
**SHORT:** `MissionProgress` (pure C#) holds `ObjectiveIndex` / `Current`; zones and interactables report completions with their ids, and `TryComplete` only accepts the id and kind of the current objective.
**DETAILED:** `MissionDirector` routes `MissionZone` entries to `NotifyReached` and interaction completions to `NotifyInteracted`. Objectives fire world events on start and complete. Scene objects check `MissionWorld.IsObjectiveTarget(id)`.
**FILES:** `S/Missions/MissionProgress.cs`, `S/Gameplay/Mission/MissionDirector.cs` · **METHOD:** `MissionProgress.TryComplete`, `Advance`, `IsCurrentTarget` · **CHAPTER:** [19](19_MISSION_SYSTEM.md)

### 16. "How does the Data Core work?"
**SHORT:** Two multi-stage interactions (a 4-stage security terminal, a 5-stage extraction uplink) run by a pure `InteractionRun`; `DataCoreChamber` animates the sleeve, the IK arms, the cable, pulses and lights from events and progress.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Mission/DataCoreChamber.cs`, `Interactable.cs`, `S/Missions/InteractionRun.cs`, `S/Editor/District/NightRunBuilder.Heist.cs` · **METHOD:** `ConfigureUplinkDefinition`, `DataCoreChamber.UpdateArms` · **CHAPTER:** [20](20_HEIST_SYSTEM.md), [22](22_DATA_CORE.md)

### 17. "What are the interaction stage types?"
**SHORT:** Auto (stay stopped), Hold (hold E; decays when released), Timing (press E in a moving window; misses cost heat and can lock you out), Sustain (answer interference with E or lose progress).
**FILES:** `S/Missions/InteractionStep.cs`, `InteractionRun.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [20](20_HEIST_SYSTEM.md)

### 18. "How does the security gate work?"
**SHORT:** A five-stage hack on a kiosk; the gate's bolts, lamps and hologram react to the hack's events; success opens the sliding gate and adds 35 % heat; a lockdown slams it and re-arms the hack.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Mission/GateLockSystem.cs`, `SecurityBarrier.cs`, `S/Editor/District/NightRunBuilder.Heist.cs` (`ConfigureGateHackDefinition`) · **CHAPTER:** [21](21_SECURITY_GATE.md)

### 19. "What is heat?"
**SHORT:** One 0–1 number for risk. Gate hacks, missed timing presses, lockouts and cameras add heat; heat raises security to Alert, shortens the escape timer and gate countdowns, and lowers the debrief grade.
**METHOD:** `MissionProgress.AddHeat` · **CHAPTER:** [23](23_HEAT_AND_LOCKDOWN.md)

### 20. "How does lockdown work?"
**SHORT:** The escape objective has security level Lockdown and fires `core.breached`, `lockdown` and `escape.start`. Every reactive object hears these: gates seal on staged countdowns, a red wave spreads at 140 m/s, signals flash, screens show warnings, sirens fade in, drones chase, traffic pulls over, the grade and mix change, and the skyway opens.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Mission/SecurityBarrier.cs`, `SecurityLightGroup.cs`, `SecurityAlarm.cs`, `S/Gameplay/City/SecurityDrones.cs`, `TrafficSignalNetwork.cs`, `LockdownScreens.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [23](23_HEAT_AND_LOCKDOWN.md)

### 21. "How do the drones follow the cars?"
**SHORT:** `SecurityDrones` launches on the counter-intrusion or lockdown; three drones tail the player and one tails each rival still running, flying a slot above and behind the car and holding a searchlight on it.
**METHOD:** `SecurityDrones.TargetFor`, `Update` · **CHAPTER:** [23](23_HEAT_AND_LOCKDOWN.md)

### 22. "How did you make the cinematic?"
**SHORT:** A Timeline generated from a shot table in code: 25 Cinemachine shot cameras animated by AnimationTracks, a custom cue track for story beats, audio tracks, a UI Toolkit overlay, and real cars driving with real physics in the actual city.
**DETAILED:** `IntroBuilder.Shots` and `Cues` → `BuildTimeline`; `IntroSceneEntry` runs it, handles Skip (jump to the title with one-shot cues applied) and Confirm (Catmull-Rom push-in matched to Car Select's first frame); `IntroCityLife` adds traffic and drones synced to the Timeline clock.
**FILES:** `S/Editor/Intro/IntroBuilder.cs`, `S/Intro/IntroSceneEntry.cs`, `IntroCue.cs`, `IntroCityLife.cs`, `D/Intro/Intro_NightRun.playable` · **CHAPTER:** [31](31_CINEMATIC_INTRO.md), [32](32_TIMELINE_AND_CAMERA.md)

### 23. "How did you make the fog / neon / rain effects?"
**SHORT:** Neon is emissive materials with HDR bloom; fog is exponential-squared distance fog plus a custom Render Graph ground-haze pass. Rain is **not implemented**: the wet look is the asphalt texture's damp patches and puddles with high smoothness reflecting baked probes.
**FILES:** `S/Rendering/GroundHazeFeature.cs`, `GroundHaze.cs`, `S/Editor/District/DistrictTextures.cs` (`Asphalt`), `Settings/Rendering/NightRun_Base.asset` · **CHAPTER:** [25](25_LIGHTING.md)–[28](28_PARTICLES_AND_VFX.md)

### 24. "How is the city lit without killing performance?"
**SHORT:** 787 lamps carry disabled lights; `LightBudget` turns on the 56 nearest (2 with shadows) with fades; everything else is emissive. No GI; baked box-projected reflection probes.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/City/LightBudget.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [25](25_LIGHTING.md)

### 25. "How does the minimap work?"
**SHORT:** A UI Toolkit element drawn with `Painter2D` from the road graph, north-up and centred on the player; only the player and rival arrows rotate; off-map targets are clamped to the rim.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Mission/CityMinimap.cs`, `MinimapProjection.cs` · **METHOD:** `MinimapProjection.ToMap`, `Heading` · **CHAPTER:** [34](34_MINIMAP.md)

### 26. "How does the HUD get its data?"
**SHORT:** `MissionDirector` pushes state into the passive `MissionHud` (UI Toolkit) through setters, only rewriting text when values change.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/Mission/MissionHud.cs`, `UI/MissionHud.uxml` · **CHAPTER:** [33](33_UI_AND_HUD.md)

### 27. "How is the grade calculated?"
**SHORT:** Out of 100: pace 40 (par 115 s → 0 at 184 s), quiet 25 × (1 − heat), position 20, clean 15 (0 at 6 hard contacts). S ≥ 90, A ≥ 75, B ≥ 60, C ≥ 45, else D.
**FILES:** `S/Missions/MissionGrade.cs` · **METHOD:** `Evaluate`, `LetterFor` · **CHAPTER:** [36](36_RESULTS_AND_DEBRIEF.md)

### 28. "What happens if you finish third?"
**SHORT:** The mission is completed but marked NOT QUALIFIED (only the first two out qualify); the qualified flag is not stored.
**METHOD:** `MissionDirector.BuildDebrief`, `MissionRecords.MarkQualified` · **CHAPTER:** [17](17_RIVAL_AI.md), [36](36_RESULTS_AND_DEBRIEF.md)

### 29. "How does the camera work?"
**SHORT:** A custom chase camera computes the pose (heading swing on slides, look-ahead, speed FOV, acceleration lag, collision, cornering bank, shake) and writes it into a Cinemachine camera; special framings for the heist, the gate and the theft beat.
**FILES:** `S/Gameplay/VehicleChaseCamera.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [38](38_CAMERA_SYSTEM.md)

### 30. "How do you handle input?"
**SHORT:** Input System actions (Driving and Menu maps) generate a typed wrapper; `PlayerDrivingInput` converts them to `DrivingInput` for the car. M toggles music globally; F3 shows telemetry in dev builds.
**FILES:** `Settings/Input/NeonRiftControls.inputactions`, `S/Input/PlayerDrivingInput.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [16](16_PLAYER_INPUT.md)

### 31. "Where is the animation implemented?"
**SHORT:** Almost entirely procedural C# (wheels, gates, bolts, arms with IK, doors, drones, lights, UI). Timeline animation only for the intro cameras; a legacy `Animation` loop for the Mech Drone.
**CHAPTER:** [37](37_ANIMATION_SYSTEMS.md)

### 32. "How did you optimise performance?"
**SHORT:** Combined meshes per block and material, cell-based culling and layer cull distances, vehicle LODs, a lamp light budget, 2 shadow cascades and Low soft shadows (−37 % GPU measured), allocation-free per-frame code, and 64 audio voices with priorities. The release macOS player averaged 38 fps at 1080p on an M3 with zero GC (measured before the latest content additions).
**FILES:** `S/Editor/Validation/RoutePerf.cs`, `GpuBench.cs`, `PerfBuild.cs` · **CHAPTER:** [39](39_PERFORMANCE.md)

### 33. "How did you test it?"
**SHORT:** 125 EditMode test cases (physics on a deterministic bench, tyres, gearbox, mission state machine, interactions, navigation, minimap, audio, intro and scene wiring) plus Play Mode validators that play the real mission through the player's interfaces.
**FILES:** `S/Tests/EditMode/`, `S/Gameplay/Mission/NightRunValidator.cs`, `S/Editor/Validation/` · **CHAPTER:** [40](40_TESTING_AND_VALIDATION.md)

### 34. "What Unity packages did you use?"
**SHORT:** URP 17.6, Input System 1.19, Cinemachine 6.6, Timeline 6.6, glTFast 6.20 (model import), Test Framework, Video module, uGUI (EventSystem only). Splines, ProBuilder and AI Navigation are installed but **not used** by the game.
**FILES:** `Packages/manifest.json` · **CHAPTER:** [03](03_TECH_STACK.md)

### 35. "Where did the assets come from, and are they licensed?"
**SHORT:** Cars, buildings and drones are Sketchfab models with recorded CC licences (one car is non-commercial); all sound effects and the fallback score are synthesised in-house; two music tracks and the traffic car pack were supplied without a recorded licence.
**FILES:** `Assets/ThirdParty/**/ATTRIBUTION.txt`, `Docs/ThirdParty.md`, `LICENSE.md` · **CHAPTER:** [44](44_ASSETS_AND_LICENSING.md)
